WHEN LAW COMPELS A GIFT, WHO DECIDES WHERE IT LANDS?

Twelve ways money meant for strangers stays close to home. Every one
of them permitted. Each carries a test you can run on any Indian
business in an afternoon, needing no paid access and no source inside.

Worked against one company's own filings, FY21 to FY26:
ASK Automotive Limited (NSE: ASKAUTOLTD).

August 2026.

===============================================================


WHAT DOES COMPULSION REACH, AND WHAT DOES IT LEAVE ALONE?

A lord in a bad year could be made to open a granary. Compulsion ran
to opening it. No writ named which village ate, or whose steward
carried grain, or who stood in a square handing out sacks and got
thanked by name.

India runs a version of that on businesses. Cross any one of three
lines -- worth above 500 crore, or takings above 1,000 crore, or
earnings above 5 crore in a year -- and you must spend near two rupees
in each hundred of average earnings across three prior years on public
good. Schools, water, work training, sport, care of sick people, care
of land and air. A board must form a committee for it. A board skipping
it must explain itself in writing inside a yearly report where anyone
can read that explanation.

So set down, with care, what compulsion reaches and what it leaves
alone. Everything after this page turns on that division.

    REACHED   -- that money leaves a business
    REACHED   -- what amount leaves, by formula
    REACHED   -- that a yearly report names each project
    REACHED   -- that unspent sums move to a separate account

    OPEN      -- whose hands carry it
    OPEN      -- what work it pays for, among a broad permitted list
    OPEN      -- which towns receive it
    OPEN      -- when across three years it gets laid out
    OPEN      -- how much counts as cost of running a carrier
    OPEN      -- whose ground a building sits on
    OPEN      -- what goods given in place of cash are worth
    OPEN      -- who sits on both a giving side and a carrying side

Four reached. Eight open.

Every pattern below lives inside one of those eight. None of them
requires breaking a rule. That is what makes them worth naming: a
reader hunting for wrongdoing finds nothing, because there is none to
find. What there is instead is a compelled outflow whose destination
nobody compels.

One more thing law permits, and it matters more than any other single
provision. A business may route this money through a trust, society or
body **it founded itself**, and such a body is spared a test applied to
strangers -- an outside carrier must show three years of track record,
while a carrier a business set up need show none. Law made a household
vehicle easier to use than an unrelated one.

A carrier registers once and receives a number that follows it after.
Those numbers run in a fixed shape: CSR followed by eight digits.


===============================================================


SO WHAT CAN BE DONE INSIDE WHAT LAW LEAVES OPEN?

Twelve patterns, grouped by which open space each one uses. Each
carries a tell -- a thing to look for -- and each tell sits in a
document anyone can pull.


-- WHO CARRIES IT --

1. ONE PAIR OF HANDS

   All of it, or near all, goes to a single body a business or its
   household founded. Nothing gets shared out among carriers chosen on
   merit, because no contest ever happens.

   Why bother: money is gone to holders of pieces whichever way it
   goes. What a household gains is a different thing from cash -- a
   standing pot it decides on, a name across many towns, seats at a
   table, and thanks in a square. None of that shows in any account.

   TELL: count distinct registry numbers across every project in a
   yearly report. A dozen carriers chosen on merit show a dozen
   numbers. One number is a finding.


2. A TRUST OLDER THAN A RULE

   Many households ran a charitable trust well before 2014, paid for
   out of household money. After 2014 a company obligation gets routed
   into that same trust. A household keeps its name on a thing that
   strangers now fund.

   TELL: set a trust's founding year against 2014. Founded well
   before, funded by a company after, with a household name attached,
   is a vehicle that changed payer while keeping owner.


3. SAME PEOPLE ON BOTH SIDES

   A committee inside a business decides where money goes. A board
   inside a carrier decides how it gets spent. Nothing stops one set
   of people holding both seats.

   TELL: take names on a giving committee from a yearly report, take
   names of trustees from a trust's own filings, and lay one list
   against another.


-- WHAT IT BUYS --

4. A ROOF ON GROUND SOMEONE OWNS

   Money may build lasting things -- a school hall, a health room, a
   water tank. Rules say such a thing must be held by a registered
   body or a public authority. Rules say nothing about whose ground it
   stands on. Strangers pay for a building; ground under it belongs to
   whoever it belonged to, worth more with a building on it.

   TELL: for any lasting thing built, ask where. Then ask who owns
   that plot.


5. GOODS VALUED BY WHOEVER GIVES THEM

   An obligation may be met in part with goods in place of cash. A
   giver sets a worth. Goods a business makes itself cost less to make
   than a price they carry.

   TELL: does a report show part of an obligation met in kind, and at
   what worth, against what those goods cost to produce.


6. A NAME ON A FIELD

   Sport, festivals and events carry banners, and thanks get spoken
   aloud in front of crowds. Rules bar spending done for advertising
   gain, and permit spending that promotes sport. A line between them
   is drawn by whoever writes a report.

   TELL: does a business name or mark appear on whatever this money
   paid for.


-- WHEN IT MOVES --

7. WORK CALLED ONGOING

   Since 2021, money not laid out inside a year cannot sit idle in a
   business. It must move out. But work declared ongoing may hold
   money in a ring-fenced account for three years. A carrier holds a
   pot. A calendar bends.

   TELL: watch balances in that separate account across years, and
   watch whether work stays ongoing for a long while.


8. LUMPY YEARS AGAINST A SMOOTH FORMULA

   An obligation runs on a three-year average, so it should climb and
   fall in gentle steps. Spending that jumps by several times from one
   year to a next has an explanation somewhere, and a reader is owed
   it.

   TELL: set yearly sums in a row and look at swings. Then look for
   words explaining them. Absence of words is itself an answer.


-- WHAT COMES BACK --

9. FIVE IN EACH HUNDRED FOR RUNNING COSTS

   A carrier may book cost of running itself against this money, up to
   five rupees in each hundred. On a spend of four and a half crore
   that is above twenty lakh a year of wages, rent and travel inside a
   trust. Should a trust employ kin, or stand on household ground,
   strangers pay those wages and that rent.

   TELL: read declared running cost each year. Sitting at a ceiling
   year after year suggests a budget being used, not a cost being
   incurred.


10. A CARRIER THAT BUYS FROM A GIVER

    Sharpest of all, and hardest to see. A trust spends its money
    buying goods or services from a business that funded it, or from
    another firm a household holds. Money leaves under one name and
    returns under another.

    TELL: a trust's own filed accounts, where obtainable, showing whom
    it paid. This one cannot be seen from a company side at all, so
    absence of evidence here means nothing either way.


-- WHERE IT LANDS --

11. TOWNS NEAR HOME

    Rules invite spending in a local area, which is sound policy and
    no criticism. An effect, though, is that standing gets built in
    places where a household already has standing, paid for by people
    who live nowhere near.

    TELL: list towns funded. Set them against where works stand and
    where a household comes from.


12. PEOPLE WHO ALSO WORK FOR YOU

    Money spent only on staff does not count toward an obligation. But
    training, schooling and health camps around a plant serve a pool a
    business hires from. A boundary is soft.

    TELL: who are named beneficiaries, and how far do they live from a
    gate.

WHAT SHOWS IN ONE COMPANY'S PAPERS, AND WHAT DOES NOT?

Now run all twelve against ASK Automotive, and be strict about which
patterns are evidenced, which are absent, and which cannot be checked
from outside. Being strict here matters more than scoring a point.

Money given away, by year, in crore of rupees, as filed:

    FY21      1.02
    FY22      3.89
    FY23      0.71
    FY24      1.29
    FY25      3.72
    FY26      4.45
             -----
    Six yrs  15.08

Work it paid for, as a yearly report lists it:

    Training for work        Jhajjar
    Schooling                Gurgaon, Rewa, Ghaziabad,
                             Prayagraj, Delhi
    Village sport            Faridabad, Sonipat, Bhiwani
    Care of land and air     Gurgaon

Nine towns. Four kinds of work. Six years.

Count registry numbers across all of it:

    One.  CSR00000244 -- AHSAAS.


PATTERN 1 -- ONE PAIR OF HANDS. Present, and plain.

Not most of that money. Not a large share. Every rupee, across four
kinds of work and nine towns, passed through one carrier.

Two separate documents carry two halves of that, and a reader should
know which carries which. A section on money given away holds that
single registry number. A list of payments to kin, running six years,
holds a line named for AHSAAS with a sum against each year -- those six
sums above, adding to 15.08 crore. Neither document says what a pair of
them says together.

Whose hands those are needs no inference. Two filings answer it in
plain words. A filing lodged with an exchange calls that trust:

    "born out of chairman & MD Kuldip Singh Rathee and his wife"

And a list of payments to kin -- a schedule every listed business must
publish, naming money that went to people connected to whoever runs it
-- carries AHSAAS under a heading covering bodies:

    "in which directors of the Company and their relatives are able
     to exercise significant influence"

So a duty owed to strangers gets discharged through a body a chairman
and his wife brought into being, which a business's own accountants
file under connected parties. Both facts are published. Neither is
buried. They sit apart, and few readers hold both at once.


PATTERN 8 -- LUMPY YEARS. Present, and unexplained.

Look again at those six sums. An obligation built on a three-year
average should walk in gentle steps. This one does not:

    FY22 3.89  ->  FY23 0.71    a fall near 82 in 100
    FY23 0.71  ->  FY25 3.72    above five times, across two years

A formula does not produce that shape on its own. Something else does
-- catch-up after a thin year, work bunching, or a carrier receiving in
one year what it spends across several. Any of those may be true. None
of them appears in words a reader can find. That is a question to put
to a board, not a charge to lay against one.


PATTERN 11 -- TOWNS NEAR HOME. Present, stated without complaint.

Of nine towns named, five sit in Haryana -- Jhajjar, Gurgaon,
Faridabad, Sonipat, Bhiwani -- and works themselves stand at Gurugram.
Remaining four spread across Delhi and two other states. Law invites
local spending and this is what local spending looks like. It is
recorded here so a reader can weigh where standing gets built and who
paid to build it.


PATTERNS 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 12 -- NOT CHECKED HERE.

Each needs a document this work has not pulled: a founding year for
AHSAAS, its trustee list, its own filed accounts, plot ownership under
anything built, running costs declared against a five in a hundred
ceiling, balances in a ring-fenced account, named beneficiaries.

Absence of a finding is not a finding. None of those nine is asserted
against this company, and a reader should treat every one as open.


WHAT CANNOT BE SAID AT ALL

Nothing above shows a rupee going astray. Those schools may be real
schools and that training may train. A trust founded by a household may
run better than a stranger would, since a founder watches a thing
carrying his name. Every step is permitted. An auditor signed without
objection. Across much of a trading world a household foundation is an
ordinary vehicle, and many Indian businesses do this.

WHAT DOES FOLLOW

A compelled outflow becomes a free choice of destination, and that
choice went one way for six years running, with no competing carrier
ever named.

Money already gone is gone to holders of pieces whichever body carries
it. What a household gains sits outside every account: a pot, a name, a
table, and thanks.

A sum tracks earnings by formula, so it swells as a business swells.
FY26 at 4.45 crore is largest yet. It is an income climbing on its own,
needing no fresh decision from anyone.


===============================================================


HOW TO RUN THIS YOURSELF, ON ANY BUSINESS, IN AN AFTERNOON

Nothing below needs paid access, training, or a source inside.

  1. Pull a yearly report. Find a section on money given away, near a
     back, among annexures.

  2. Count distinct registry numbers, shape CSR00000000. One number
     across many projects is a finding. A dozen is not.

  3. Take whatever name carries that number and search a list of
     payments to kin in that same report. A name appearing there means
     a business has told you itself that a carrier is connected.

  4. Search a whole report for that carrier's name. Some filing often
     states who founded it, in words as plain as those quoted above.

  5. Set yearly sums in a row. Gentle steps match a formula. Swings of
     several times need words, and if no words exist, that is worth
     asking about.

  6. Read declared running cost against a five in a hundred ceiling.
     Sitting at a ceiling year after year is worth a question.

  7. List towns funded. Set them against where works stand.

  8. Look up that carrier in a registry of trusts and societies. A
     founding year before 2014, and trustee names matching a board,
     each answer a question above.

Should steps two, three and four land on one name, you have a first
pattern. What you do after that is a separate matter, and this page
takes no view on it.
