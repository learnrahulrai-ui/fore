FOREIGN SUBSIDIARY SETTLEMENT TRICK — WORKING NOTES
=====================================================
Status: raw findings, not yet verified against primary filings for any
named company. Nothing below should be attributed to a specific promoter
family until traced to that family's own RPT schedule, ODI/APR filings,
and the foreign entity's local registry record.

THE PROBLEM THIS SOLVES, FOR THE PROMOTER
==========================================

A child emigrates — marriage, a graduate degree that turns into a work
visa, eventually PR. The family now wants to fund a foreign lifestyle,
foreign real estate, maybe a path to citizenship, at a scale that
personal after-tax money can't comfortably reach. Two doors are open:
move personal money out, or move company money out. Personal money hits
a wall almost immediately, which is what pushes the whole design toward
the company route.

THE WALL: WHY IT HAS TO BE THE COMPANY, NOT THE PERSON
========================================================

FEMA's Liberalised Remittance Scheme caps what an individual can send
abroad at USD 250,000 per financial year. A promoter family of four
(self, spouse, two children still India-resident) could in theory
combine limits, but that's still low single-digit millions a year — not
enough to buy a London flat, fund a Delaware/Cayman holding structure,
and pay a "salary" that reads as normal for a Western capital.
[Unverified — check current FEMA Master Direction on LRS for the live cap
before citing this figure in any published report.]

A company moving money abroad answers to a completely different regime:
Reserve Bank of India Overseas Direct Investment rules, which let an
Indian entity fund a foreign joint venture or wholly-owned subsidiary up
to a large multiple of net worth — historically described in financial
press as up to 400% under the automatic route, with RBI approval
available above that. [Unverified — ODI rules were restructured in
August 2022; confirm the current financial-commitment ceiling before
using this number as fact.] Net worth for a mid-cap listed company is
hundreds of crores. Four hundred percent of that dwarfs what any
individual's LRS quota could ever move.

That gap — personal remittance capped near a quarter-million dollars a
year, corporate overseas investment capped at multiples of a company's
entire net worth — is the actual reason the vehicle is a "subsidiary" or
"JV" and not a wire transfer. It isn't disguise for its own sake. It's
regulatory arbitrage: pick the channel with the bigger pipe.

WHAT HAPPENS ONCE THE MONEY IS ALREADY OFFSHORE
==================================================

Getting money out via ODI is the hard, scrutinized part — it needs a
board resolution, an AD bank, RBI filings (Form FC), and an annual
performance report proving the subsidiary is a going concern. Once it
clears that gate, though, the intra-entity spending is far less watched
from India, because nobody downstream is auditing "is this consulting
fee reasonable for a Singapore entity" the way SEBI's related-party
regime would audit the same question for a domestic transaction. So the
design front-loads all the difficulty into a single, legal, well-
precedented step (fund the subsidiary), and pushes all the actual
benefit-transfer into a second step that happens entirely outside
Indian jurisdiction.

Inside the subsidiary, the conversion to personal benefit takes a small
number of recurring shapes: a title (CEO / Head of International
Business / Regional Director) that gives the payout an employment
rationale instead of a gift rationale; an above-market salary or
"consultancy fee" sized to the family's needs rather than to the role;
a property booked as office or guest house that the child actually
lives in, sometimes later sold to the child at book value once
depreciation has brought book value well under market; and, where the
Indian parent exports, a transfer-pricing gap — sell to the subsidiary
cheap, let the subsidiary resell at the real market price, and let the
margin sit in the entity the child controls rather than in the entity
the public owns.

A second motive rides along with the extraction motive and shouldn't be
missed: several of the destination jurisdictions (UAE, Portugal, and
others at various times) grant residency or a path to citizenship for
property purchases above a threshold. A "corporate office" purchase
sized to hit that threshold can be doing two jobs — sheltering money and
buying the child status — at once. And because India currently levies no
inheritance or estate duty, pre-positioning wealth abroad as the child's
own salary/property/equity, rather than leaving it inside Indian company
shares to be inherited later, is also a hedge against India ever
reintroducing estate duty, which comes up in budget speculation
periodically.

WHY THE FRONT END STAYS BORING ON PURPOSE
============================================

Every design choice on the Indian side is aimed at making the parent
company's own disclosures look unremarkable. The child is not made a KMP
or director of the Indian listed entity — only of the foreign
subsidiary — because SEBI LODR Regulation 23's related-party machinery
triggers off Indian-entity KMP status and relatives thereof; skip that
appointment and the child's foreign compensation never has to appear in
the Indian company's own related-party note. Capital moves to the
subsidiary as recurring "working capital" or "capex" tranches instead of
one lump sum, because a steady drip reads as ordinary business funding
where a single large transfer reads as a flag. And the subsidiary is
kept technically "operational" — some invoices, some revenue, however
thin — because a fully dormant shell is a much easier thing for an
auditor or RBI to question than a real-looking business that simply
underperforms.

WHERE THE TRAIL SURVIVES ANYWAY
==================================

None of this is undetectable from India; it's just detectable from a
different document than the ones most retail analysts pull.

The clean, no-foreign-access signal sits inside the Indian consolidated
financial statements already: segment or subsidiary-level disclosure
usually shows each subsidiary's standalone revenue and profit. A foreign
subsidiary that has existed three-plus years, has absorbed repeated
capital infusions, and still shows revenue near zero is a ratio anyone
can compute from the annual report already in hand — capital in versus
revenue out, tracked year over year.

One step further requires pulling the RBI-facing filings: the Form FC
at the time of each investment and the Annual Performance Report the
Indian company must keep submitting to its AD bank to prove the
subsidiary is real. These aren't typically public in the way BSE/NSE
filings are, but their existence means the company itself is on record,
annually, asserting the subsidiary is a going concern — which is a
useful thing to test against.

The step that actually breaks the story open is the foreign entity's
own local registry. UK Companies House publishes director filings and,
for smaller entities, abbreviated accounts, effectively free. Singapore's
ACRA/BizFile exposes officer and shareholder data behind a small paid
lookup. UAE entities are the hardest — free-zone and mainland
structures both tolerate nominee arrangements that can keep beneficial
ownership opaque even when the registry itself is nominally searchable.
Cross-referencing the Indian ODI filing's declared entity name against
that foreign registry — does the "Head of International Business" match
a director listing, does the "corporate office" address match a
residential unit, does the JV partner in the registry match anyone
connected to the promoter family by marriage or otherwise — is the move
that turns a suspicious ratio into a traceable structure.

CASE PATTERN ON RECORD (CITE, DON'T EXTEND)
==============================================

Vijay Mallya / United Spirits / Kingfisher is the cleanest publicly
adjudicated-in-progress example of the underlying mechanism, though the
specific channel alleged by the Enforcement Directorate was Diageo
settlement proceeds rather than an operating subsidiary: reporting
(Moneycontrol, citing the ED's dossier) describes roughly $40 million
moved to his three children, which the ED characterized as diversion of
funds that creditors had a prior claim to. Useful as proof the
mechanism — company-linked money reaching adult children abroad, probed
years after the fact — gets investigated and reported on when it
surfaces, not as a template to extend to any other named family without
that family's own primary documents in hand.

The Pandora Papers reporting on offshore entities connected to
prominent Indian families (Caravan Magazine's coverage is the citation
already in hand) is the same caution in a harder form: leaked-document
journalism established the existence of the entities and the family
connection; it did not establish the source of capital or any specific
onward use. Treat it as evidence that offshore-entity-plus-family-member
structures exist and get reported, not as a second worked example of
the settlement trick specifically — that inferential leap isn't
supported by what the leak actually showed.

HOW THIS SLOTS INTO THE TECHNIQUE CATALOG ALREADY BUILT
===========================================================

The earlier working list for this project (Reddit r/IndiaInvestments +
ValuePickr synthesis) already covers related-party loans/write-offs,
shell-company transactions, and inflated capex as domestic categories.
The foreign-subsidiary settlement trick isn't a new category so much as
those same three mechanisms — related-party payout, a not-quite-shell
entity, and capex sized to move money rather than build capacity —
relocated across a border specifically to (a) use the ODI pipe instead
of the LRS pipe, and (b) put the benefit-transfer step in a
jurisdiction where SEBI's related-party disclosure regime has no reach.
Same skeleton as techniques 1, 3, and 4 on the original list; the
foreign wrapper is what changes which regulator is watching, and it
substitutes "settling a child abroad" for "extracting cash domestically"
as the end use.
