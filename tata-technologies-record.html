Why does one customer finishing explain what came after?

Tata Technologies Limited. Slips first sold to a public in November 2023. Compiled 17 November 2026. A note at a foot sets out five things earlier versions of this page got wrong.

A man who hires out bullocks knows a difference between two kinds of customer. One takes a pair every season, year on year, and a man can plan around him. Another hires forty bullocks for a single job — clearing a new tract — pays a great deal for eighteen months, and then that tract is cleared and he is gone.

Neither is a bad customer. But a man whose income leans on a second kind owns a different business from a man whose income leans on a first, and only one of them can tell you what next year looks like. Everything on this page follows from that difference, and from where a sale of slips landed inside one such job.

This company does not sell goods off a shelf. It takes on whole vehicle programmes for makers who have no platform of their own — design, engineering, manufacturing setup, all of it. Management's own phrase for these is "mega full vehicle programs". A customer with no car of his own hands over everything, a team is built for him, and when that car exists, a job is finished.

Two things follow by construction, before any judgement about anybody. Money from one customer is enormous. And it ends on a date somebody wrote down at a start.

So a count of customers is a first thing to look for. Offer papers filed 13 November 2023 give it.

Five customers, seven rupees in every ten of a main trade. Three were named: a parent, a parent's British arm, and VinFast, a Vietnamese maker of electric cars, a customer since 2018. A rating agency later put VinFast at about 22 in each hundred of everything coming in during FY23, falling to 11 by FY24.

Hold those together and a position is plain. Of ten rupees, seven came from five hands. Two of those hands belonged to a same family as this company. And one outside hand was worth above two rupees on its own, on a job with an end date already written.

What was a buyer told, and what did a buyer pay?

A sale ran 22 to 24 November 2023 at ₹500 a slip, taking ₹3,042.5 crore. No new slips were cut, so nothing reached this company's own account. Money went from thousands of buyers into three holders' hands — a parent taking 11.41 in a hundred of this company, Alpha TC Holdings 2.4, Tata Capital Growth Fund 1.2.

What was a buyer told about a risk? As it turns out, all of it.

Printed, published, and open to anyone before bidding closed: •  Offer papers named VinFast as a main customer, and named dependence
    on five customers as a risk, at 70.92 per cent of services work.

•  VinFast had sold its own slips in America that August, and its price had
    since fallen above ninety in each hundred from peak.

•  Analysts wrote about both, in print, before a book closed.

A book was bid 69.43 times over. Institutions bid 203 times their portion. A first trade on 30 November opened at ₹1,200, 140 in a hundred above an allotted price, and touched ₹1,400 that day.

Nobody was deceived about what might go wrong. It was written down, and a market paid 140 in a hundred above an allotted price anyway. That is a different complaint from concealment, and a harder one, because no rule mends it.

Who knew that job was ending, and when?

Here a page must be careful, because earlier versions of it were careless. This company did not lose VinFast. Nobody was sacked and no contract was torn up. A job finished. Management's own account, on a call in May 2024:

"Revenue from the mega full vehicle programs with VinFast significantly declined in Q3 and further in Q4, as we ramped down our deployed capacity... As head count is concerned in Q3 and in Q4, we've calibrated to accommodate the runoff that we've seen at VinFast."

Forty bullocks came home. A man did not lose a customer; a tract was cleared. "We ramped down our deployed capacity" is a company reducing, on purpose, to match a job ending. "Calibrated" head count is a payroll cut to fit.

Which raises a question this whole page exists to put.

When is a job's ending known?

A turnkey vehicle programme is not weather. It carries a schedule written at a start, because a customer pays against milestones and a team is built against them. Somebody inside knows when forty bullocks come home, and knows it long before they walk through a gate. A summary of that same May 2024 call reports management saying they had anticipated a VinFast ramp-down twelve months earlier, which would place that knowledge around May 2023.

A sale of slips priced in November 2023.

This is not established and must not be treated as established. That twelve-month line comes from a third party's summary of a call, not from a transcript read here, and a transcript is public and would settle it in one sentence. Nor would it show wrongdoing if confirmed: offer papers named VinFast and named concentration as a risk, which is what offer papers are for. What it would settle is narrower and still worth knowing — whether a forward hole in revenue was visible inside a company while a price was being set outside it.

Settled by: a Q4 FY24 earnings call transcript, on this company's own investor pages.

Why was that job not followed by another from a same customer? A separate trouble, set down here only as a reason a customer stopped spending. VinFast's own slips fell above ninety in a hundred. American safety regulators opened a review of its VF8 concerning a lane keeping system — a different thing from chassis and suspension. A family of four died in a crash in California in April 2024 and a wrongful death suit has been filed; local police cited speed as a possible factor and no authority has settled a cause. A former engineer's account of component failures, and of his later dismissal, was reported by BBC in December 2024; an employment tribunal has not concluded and both companies declined to comment. None of that is alleged against this company here, and no authority has linked any of it to work this company did. It matters for one reason only: a customer in that condition does not commission a next vehicle, and a next vehicle is what would have replaced forty bullocks.

A best harvest was FY24 — a first full year after that sale, and a year a run-off was under way. FY25 growth fell to one in a hundred. FY26 profit fell near a fifth, to below FY23, a last full year a buyer saw in offer papers.

Two things cut against reading this as a business in trouble, and both are printed at a same size as everything else. Work outside VinFast kept growing — management put it at ten in a hundred on that call, though other accounts of a same period say thirty, so a figure is in dispute. And cash tells a kinder story than profit: in FY26 this business generated ₹776 crore of cash against ₹547 crore of reported profit, with ₹743 crore left after everything spent on plant. A harvest sold for less, yet more money ended in a box than a ledger said was earned.

But losing an outside customer did one thing no replacement fixed. VinFast was a large customer not of this company's own family. With that job gone, a share of work coming from a parent and a parent's British arm rose. A company that told buyers it was reducing dependence on its own family became more dependent on it — and in a quarter to September 2026, a largest customer was struck by a cybersecurity incident and contributed nothing for part of it.

Across six months before, a slip ran ₹507 to ₹891, up 75.7 in a hundred, while profit fell in each of two quarters — 204 to 181 to 171 crore. Two months after, a slip is ₹648.

Why hand slips to one buyer instead of selling into open trading? Push 21 lakh slips into a market and a price falls while you sell, so a seller pays for his own exit. Hand them over at a price agreed beforehand and a price does not move. Only buyers large enough to take that in one piece are houses holding money villagers send in every month, and such money must be put somewhere each month. Monthly savings are not incidental to an arrangement of this kind. They are what makes it possible.

What this page claims, and what it refuses to.

Claims: five customers were seven rupees in ten of a main trade, and it was printed. One of those five was a finite job worth above a fifth of everything, printed too. Management says it ramped its own capacity down as that job ended. Growth fell to one in a hundred a year after. Losing an outside customer raised a share coming from this company's own family. Every holder who entered before a public did has left, two at a local high a month after a run-off was described. And on one day four fund houses paid ₹164.85 crore to one man, after a rise reported harvests did not support.

Refuses: that anybody was deceived — a risk was named in offer papers. That a job ending was a loss, a sacking or a failure — it was a programme completing. That anybody lifted a price in 2026 — a rise against falling harvests is a fact without an author. That this company's work caused any safety trouble at a customer — no authority has found that and this page does not suggest it. That a man who sold in 2026 gained a great deal — his cost is unknown and cannot be worked out here. And that fund houses were wrong — ₹785 may look cheap in ten years.
Corrections, kept in view and not edited away.

Earlier versions of this page said FY24 figures could not be found, and used that absence to claim a fall in profit could not be dated. They were available, and show profit peaked in FY24.

They said no figure for cash generated could be obtained, and declared a funding test un-runnable. Every figure was available, and it comes out in this company's favour.

They put a parent's holding at 53.09 per cent from a subtraction of mine. A filed figure is 55.17 per cent.

They said a link between a man reducing his holding and funds raising theirs could not be established, and named a record that would settle it. That record is public and settles it. A document I called missing was one I never opened.

And they wrote this story as a customer being lost at a time of a sale. He was not lost. A job finished on a schedule, which is a different thing and, for anyone pricing slips, a more interesting one.

A cause of all five was one habit: a pattern found at another company was carried here, and searching stopped once figures fit that pattern.
