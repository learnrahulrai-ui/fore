Three years on from a listing, how do earnings compare with what buyers were shown?

Tata Technologies Limited. Listed 30 November 2023. Worked from figures reported in public, with what could not be checked kept apart from what could. Compiled 3 November 2026.

Stated before anything else, because it governs how much weight any line here carries. A company's own FY26 annual report sits behind a server that refuses requests, so not one figure below was read from a filing first-hand. Every one comes from a press report, a broker note quoted in a press report, or a data service. Marks are used throughout: ● two or more sources agree, ● one source only, ● sources conflict. Arithmetic done on this page is shown in full, so a reader can redo it or reject it.

What do buyers at each door have to show for it?

Start with what buyers were handed in November 2023, since every judgement afterwards runs against it.

A share sale ran 22 to 24 November 2023 at ₹500 a share, raising ₹3,042.5 crore. ● It was wholly a sale of shares already held — no fresh shares were created, so none of that money reached this company. Three holders sold: Tata Motors 11.41 per cent, Alpha TC Holdings 2.4 per cent, Tata Capital Growth Fund 1.2 per cent, near fifteen per cent between them. ● Demand ran to 69.43 times shares on offer, and institutions bid 203.41 times their portion against retail at about 16.5. ●

Six weeks earlier something else had happened, and it belongs beside that price. In October 2023 Tata Motors sold 9.9 per cent to buyers led by TPG, with a Ratan Tata endowment taking 0.9 of it, for ₹1,613.7 crore. ●

What that October sale implies a share cost, worked out, not quoted: (1)  Tata Motors sold 46,275,000 shares in a public offer = 11.41 per cent
      so shares in issue = 46,275,000 ÷ 0.1141 = 40.56 crore

(2)  9.9 per cent of 40.56 crore = 4.02 crore shares

(3)  ₹1,613.7 crore ÷ 4.02 crore shares = ₹402 a share

(4)  ₹402 in October, ₹500 in November = a step up of 24.4 per cent in six weeks

Assumed in (1): that 46,275,000 and 11.41 per cent describe one and the same holding. Both come from offer papers reported in press. If either is rounded, every figure after it moves.

That is worth stating without ornament: three years after a listing, profit is below what it was in a final year printed in offer papers, while revenue has grown a quarter. Whatever grew did not reach earnings.

Listing day close is reported three ways — ₹1,313 as a last trade, ₹1,314.25, and ₹1,327 — so that row carries a conflict mark and ₹1,314 is used. Price on 3 November 2026 is reported as ₹648 by one data service and ₹656.45 as a close on 2 November by another; a lower is used, and using a higher moves each row by about one point.

A listing opened at 140 per cent above an offer price and touched ₹1,400 that day. Anyone who bought a company on a day it became buyable has lost about half. Anyone allotted at ₹500 is ahead by near thirty per cent.

Thirty per cent sounds like a win, so put a holding period on it, which is where it stops sounding like one.

Offer price against a floor of ten per cent a year, which is what these pages use as a real cost of holding money in India: 30 Nov 2023 to 3 Nov 2026  =  2.93 years

₹500 → ₹648  over 2.93 years  =  9.27 per cent a year

A floor of ten per cent a year on ₹500  =  500 × 1.102.93  =  ₹661

Actual ₹648 against a floor of ₹661  =  short by ₹13

Which is a near miss, and gets reported as a near miss. Thirteen rupees on 648 is two per cent, inside what a different end date or a different inflation figure would move. What it is not is a gain worth a listing that was bid 69 times over.

What cannot be checked from here, and what would settle it?

Seven items below could each change a reading above. None is a finding. Each carries a document that would answer it.

  1. Whether cash from trading paid for anything. No figure for cash generated by operations, money spent on plant and equipment, or dividends paid appears anywhere read here. So a test these pages apply to every company — did a business fund itself, or did it borrow and raise — has not been run on this company at all, and nothing on this page should be read as having run it.
    Settled by: a cash flow statement, FY23 through FY26.
  2. How much of revenue comes from a parent and from Jaguar Land Rover. Reported figures run from 23 per cent for Jaguar Land Rover alone, to a quarter, to 30 to 35 per cent for both together, to about 40 per cent in FY23, to about 50 per cent in FY24. A FY23 figure and a FY24 figure cannot both be right as stated. One note says a share stood near 75 per cent in 2014, so a direction over a decade is down. A level now is unknown.
    Settled by: a client concentration disclosure in an annual report, which exists and could not be reached.
  3. What a parent holds today. A holding was 74.4 per cent before October 2023. Subtracting 9.9 and 11.41 gives 53.09 per cent. That is a subtraction I did, resting on a 74.4 figure being current at that date and both sales completing in full. No filed holding figure for 2026 was found.
    Settled by: a quarterly holding filing.
  4. Whether a parent sold more after that listing. Not found either way. Absence of a report is not absence of a sale.
    Settled by: holding filings across twelve quarters, and bulk deal records.
  5. What FY24 was. Missing from everything read. Without it, a fall in profit cannot be dated, and a reading that growth slowed after a listing, and not during it, is unsupported.
    Settled by: FY24 results, which are published.
  6. Why profit fell while revenue rose. A company's own report says profitability moderated. Causes — pricing, pay, mix of work, a currency, an acquisition — are not established here, and a margin falling 4.21 points over three years is large enough that a cause matters more than a fact.
    Settled by: a management discussion section and a run of earnings calls.
  7. What a quarter grew by. For a quarter to March 2026, revenue of ₹1,572.22 crore is reported both as 22.3 per cent above a year before and as 15.1 per cent above a quarter before. Those measure different things and one of them is being quoted as if it were a second. Profit of ₹204.17 crore is reported up 8 per cent on a year, while a separate adjusted figure of ₹162.50 crore is reported up 20.3 per cent on a quarter.
    Settled by: an exchange filing for that quarter.
And a shape worth naming as a shape, not as a finding. A sale of shares already held, raising nothing for a company; a final year of accounts in offer papers that turns out to be a high point for profit; and a buyer on listing day holding half of what was paid. That is a pattern these pages have recorded elsewhere, and recognising a pattern is a reason to go and check, never a reason to conclude. Two things cut against reading it as one here: a slowing may be a condition of a whole industry and not anything about this company, and nobody has shown a seller knew what a buyer did not. Neither of those is settled on this page either.
