What is on record about this company, and what have I only assumed?

Reliance Industries. A record I keep for myself, so that months from now, when somebody publishes a case for buying it, I can check my own reasoning against what I knew at a time of deciding. Compiled 3 November 2026.

Two things sit on this page and they are kept apart on purpose.

First, what filings and press reports record, with a date against each line and a mark for how well each one is checked. Second, and in its own section, what I have assumed while reading those lines and have not established. That second list exists because I wrote a conclusion from this record before I had earned it, and caught myself only when somebody asked me to show a derivation I could not show.

So a rule for this page, which costs little and saves a great deal. Nothing moves from a lower section to a higher one until a primary document supports it.

How each line is marked. ● two or more sources agree   ● one secondary source only   ● sources conflict, or unchecked

No line here is a primary filing read first-hand. Every one is a press report, a broker note quoted in a press report, or a commentary piece. For a record kept as a reminder that is enough. For anything published as a finding it is not, and each one would need its annual report, scheme document or exchange filing pulled first.

What have I assumed without establishing, and what would change my mind?

Six things below read as though they follow from a table above. None of them does. Each is written out so that a reader, me included, cannot mistake one for a finding, and each carries what would settle it.

  1. That money from those thirteen investors paid off that ₹1.08 lakh crore. A table records two separate facts: borrowings moved to Reliance Industries, and Reliance Industries later cashed in ₹1.29 lakh crore of preference shares. Nothing recorded connects one to another. Nil net debt is a position, not a repayment — hold ₹100 of borrowings against ₹200 of cash and you have it without repaying a rupee, which a source quoted above says in as many words.
    Settled by: a borrowings schedule across FY20 and FY21 showing whether that ₹1.08 lakh crore fell, stayed, or was refinanced.
  2. That cash from trading never paid any of it. I asserted this. I have not read a single cash flow statement for any year on this page. An assertion about a quantity nobody has looked at is worth nothing, and it was worse for being dressed as a test I had run elsewhere.
    Settled by: cash generated from operations against capital spending against repayments, FY19 through FY26, from audited accounts.
  3. That borrowings paid down in 2026 are what was left of 2019's. I wrote "remaining", which smuggles in a continuity I have no evidence for. Those ₹27,500 crore of foreign borrowings may have been raised in full after 2021.
    Settled by: Reliance Jio Infocomm's own borrowing schedule by year of drawdown.
  4. That $14.1 billion and ₹1.08 lakh crore describe one transfer. I treated two sources as corroborating each other by assuming they spoke of a same event. They may describe two. I also converted at ₹75 to a dollar without saying so.
    Settled by: a scheme document naming amounts and dates for each transfer.
  5. That entities connected to promoters have been passing stock to small holders since 2020. I looked and found nothing supporting this. A nearest matter on record is that 2007 case — and a Supreme Court set aside its findings of fraud and manipulation on 29 May 2026. Asserting it now would mean asserting what a highest court declined to find.
    Settled by: quarterly holding filings, entity by entity, 2020 to date. Until then this stays out.
  6. That a recent statement promises repayment of borrowings. Heard, not found.
    Settled by: a transcript or filing carrying it.
And a caution about a frame, which belongs here and not in a table. It is tempting to read a run of dates as a run of character, and to say that people who run companies do not change. That sentence cannot be checked against any document, it says nothing a filing can confirm or deny, and in India it carries a risk no finding on this page carries. A record of what was done, dated, is worth keeping. A verdict on who somebody is turns a record into an opinion and drags everything factual down with it. So it stays off.

Which brings a last question, and it is a point of this whole page: so what did I decide, and on what? I have not bought this company. Three reasons stood behind that, and each one needs a line saying what would end it, or in six months I will have no way to grade myself.

ReasonWhat would end it
A measure of borrowings was struck on a basis its own analysts rejected, and a prior year's figure for that measure was stated two ways in two reports. A reason to hold a parent was exposure to Jio. Jio Platforms now trades on its own, so that exposure arrives through a parent at whatever discount a market applies to holding companies, and no longer first-hand.A holding company discount that does not open, or a route by which a parent's holders get direct exposure.

A thing to notice about those three: not one depends on anything in a section above it. That is deliberate. A reason that rests on an assumption I have not established is a reason that can collapse without a company doing anything, and I would sooner hold three I can defend than six I cannot.

And a correction kept in plain view, since a value of this page is that it records how a mistake was handled. A first draft of this record closed with a sentence claiming borrowings had been retired twice by outside money and never by cash from trading. Four parts of that were assumed, none derived. Items one, two, three and four above are what that sentence turned into once each part was pulled apart. Nothing was deleted; it was moved to where it belonged.
