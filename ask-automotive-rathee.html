What happened, in the order it happened

ASK Automotive listed in November 2023, entirely via Offer For Sale — no fresh capital reached the company, every rupee of the ₹833.91 crore issue went straight to selling shareholders. Promoter+group holding after listing: 85%, against a 15% public float.

Eight months later, on July 29, 2024, an analyst asked directly about that 85% holding. Joint MD Aman Rathee didn't dodge it — he laid out the whole plan: "we have diluted 15% and for the next 10% as per the SEBI guidelines, we still have around 2 years to dilute it... we will surely inform you as and when we descend." Which is exactly what happened, faster than promised: 6.05% sold two months later, then a further 4.00% sixteen months after that — 10.05% against the 10% he'd named, completed in 16 months instead of the 2 years he'd flagged.

The first tranche came on September 26-27, 2024. Kuldip Singh Rathee personally: 40.23% → 34.79% → 34.18% over the two-day OFS. 11,927,127 shares, ₹539.5 crore by the primary filing (two separate news aggregators, summarizing the same event, gave ₹487.9 crore and ₹516.4 crore — disagreeing with each other and with the filing). The price genuinely fell for it — ₹458.8 down to ₹402.9 over the following three weeks, a real ~12% decline. No single fund in the tracked mutual-fund data absorbed this one in a block; whoever bought, the market visibly had to digest the supply.

And yet the very next earnings call, five weeks later, said nothing about it. Neither did the one after that, or the one after that — six consecutive calls, spanning both this sale and the next one, with not one analyst asking and not one word from management.

Meanwhile a new risk entered the story. News broke in June 2025 of a proposal to make ABS compulsory on every two-wheeler, not just the larger ones — a real threat to a company whose scale is built on the cheaper alternative. The very next call, three analysts pressed him on it. He called it "sudden," and when cornered, gave a number: ₹230 crore worst case, "the worst number." Then deferred everything else to the next call. Three months on, the answer softened to genuine-sounding uncertainty — plausible, since even the OEMs reportedly couldn't say either, matching reports the government was considering a delay. But by the following call it hardened into an outright refusal — "we will not talk on this hypothetical question" — and by the one after that, a year after the topic first came up, it was still exactly that: "a very hypothetical question... I can't answer on that." Never answered once, even as the regulatory deadline it concerned had already passed.

Twenty-five days after that last soft-uncertainty answer, on November 25, 2025, the second sale went through — a single bulk deal this time, not a two-day OFS. Kuldip Singh Rathee: 34.18% → 30.18%. Promoter+group: 78.95% → 74.95%, just under the minimum public float floor. 7,885,704 shares, ₹372 crore, at ₹473, to SBI Mutual Fund. Two SBI schemes combined to 7,908,563 shares by December — a 100.29% match to the sale — then held that exact share count, unchanged to the digit, through April, May and June 2026. The price didn't react at all: ₹480.7, ₹497.35, ₹479.15, ₹465.65, ₹490.3 — flat, sideways churn, nothing like the organic decline the first sale produced. Screener's own DII shareholding row independently confirms it, jumping 4.82% to 9.13% in exactly this quarter, having barely moved through the first one. And the earnings call that followed — the first chance anyone had to ask what just happened — said nothing about it either.

The one window where the public won outright is the one before any of his selling started. Every window that begins on one of his own sale dates has lost to gold on every horizon resolved so far, and lost to real inflation on all but one.
